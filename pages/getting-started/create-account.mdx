# Create an Account

Your Superhero account **is** a keypair on the æternity blockchain: a public address that starts
with `ak_` and a private key that only you hold. Creating an account means generating that keypair;
logging in means proving you still have it. You own it outright, with nothing to sign up for and
nobody else holding a copy.

## What self-sovereign means

| You own | What that means |
| --- | --- |
| Your identity | Your address is your identity; a `.chain` name makes it readable |
| Your access | You keep a recovery phrase, or a passkey, or both; they take the place of a password |
| Your recovery | Your backup is the restore path; see [Backup & Recovery](/wallets/backup-and-recovery) |
| Your history | Posts and transactions are permanent on chain and verifiable by anyone |
| Your key | Nothing leaves your device |

Nobody can freeze the account, nobody can post as you, and no company sits between you and the
chain. Your backup is what keeps that control in your hands, so do the backup step first.

## Three ways to get an account

| Path | Where | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Create in the Superhero app** | The Connect dialog on superhero.com | First-time users who want to be in the app in one step |
| **Create in Superhero Wallet** | The extension, mobile app or web wallet | One wallet across several chains and æpps |
| **Import an existing account** | Any of the above | Anyone who already holds an æternity seed phrase |

All three produce the same kind of account on the same derivation path. The same seed phrase gives
you the same addresses in every one of them.

## Create in the Superhero app

Press **Connect**. The dialog offers a passkey (the built-in wallet), Superhero Wallet, and an AI
agent setup.

### With a passkey

Choose **Use a passkey**. One ceremony does everything: Face ID, Touch ID, a fingerprint or your
device PIN, and a wallet exists.

The phrase is **derived** from the passkey's own output at a fixed salt, so it can be re-derived on
any device the passkey reaches, and the app skips the seed-phrase screen. It takes you straight to
the mandatory **recovery code** screen.

Use a passkey that **syncs** (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager): on this path the passkey is
your backup, and a synced one reaches every device you sign in on. A device-bound credential such as
Windows Hello stays with its device, so if that is what your device offers, choose the recovery
phrase path instead.

If the option is greyed out, this device or browser cannot create a platform passkey; use a
recovery phrase instead. If tapping it does nothing at all, see
[Troubleshooting](/wallets/troubleshooting).

### With a recovery phrase

Choose **Use a recovery phrase**. Twelve words you write down and keep yourself; works on any
device, with or without biometrics.

1. **The phrase is shown.** Write it down by hand. Do not screenshot it.
2. **The phrase is verified.** You re-enter the words. Nothing is stored until this passes.
3. **You set a passphrase.** It encrypts the wallet on this device. The app scores it locally and
   requires a genuinely unguessable one, so a numeric PIN is rejected. Take the **generated
   five-word passphrase** unless you have a reason not to. The passphrase is fixed for this
   wallet; to change it, recreate the wallet from the phrase.
4. **The wallet is created** and your first address is derived.
5. **A device passkey is offered.** Optional. With it you stop typing the passphrase for every
   signature.
6. **A recovery code is shown once.** Mandatory. It is a 128-bit code that opens this device's
   wallet if your passphrase is forgotten or your passkey stops working. Save it somewhere other
   than where you put the phrase.

### Import an existing wallet

Offered **in the installed app** (Add to Home Screen / installed PWA). In a plain browser tab, use
**Connect** instead, which hands signing to your extension or wallet app and never asks for your
secret.

Imported phrases are checked against the BIP39 wordlist **and checksum** before anything is stored,
so a mistyped word is caught before it can create a different, empty wallet.

### Restore a passkey wallet on a new device

Choose **Restore a wallet you created with a passkey**. The ceremony derives the wallet your passkey
implies and **shows you the address before saving anything**, together with a check of whether that
address has on-chain history. If it reports no activity, stop: you probably picked the wrong
passkey. Details in [Backup & Recovery](/wallets/backup-and-recovery).

### Coming back later

If this device already has a wallet, Connect shows either *"Your wallet is ready"* or *"Finish
setting up your wallet"*. The second appears when the recovery code was never enrolled, for
example after a reload mid-flow. Finish it: on a passkey wallet, that code is what gets you back in
after a passkey rekey.

## Create in Superhero Wallet

Install the [extension](/wallets/superhero-wallet-extension) or the
[mobile app](/wallets/superhero-wallet-mobile), or open
[wallet.superhero.com](https://wallet.superhero.com/) with nothing to install. Choose **Create new
wallet**, pick the blockchain for your first account, and you land on the dashboard. Then return to
superhero.com and press Connect.

This is the right choice if you want one wallet across æternity, Bitcoin, Ethereum and the rest, or
if you will use other æternity æpps.

### Back up the seed phrase on day one

Superhero Wallet reminds you with a "Please backup your seed phrase!" notification until the phrase
is marked backed up. Do it before funding anything:

1. **Settings → Seed phrase → Show seed phrase.** Revealing it requires re-authentication:
   biometrics on the mobile app, your password on the extension and web, or an explicit
   confirmation if you have no password set.
2. **Write the words down.** Two copies, offline.
3. **Verify seed phrase.** Your words appear shuffled; tap them back into the correct order.

Also set up **Settings → Secure Login**: it turns on password protection if you skipped it, sets
the auto-lock interval, and lets you change the password later.

## Create an account for an agent

An agent needs a raw key it can sign with unattended:

```bash
node scripts/superhero-wallet.mjs generate
```

That prints an address and an `AE_PRIVATE_KEY` **once**. Save it, export it, and never commit it:

```bash
export AE_PRIVATE_KEY=<the_key_it_printed>
node scripts/superhero-wallet.mjs address
```

Use a dedicated account funded with a capped amount. See
[Agent Wallets and Keys](/agents/wallet-and-keys) and [Wallet Security](/wallets/security).

## If you clear browser storage

An account created in the Superhero app lives encrypted in this browser's IndexedDB for the
superhero.com origin. **Clearing site data removes that copy**, and so does Safari's tracking
prevention after roughly seven days without interaction. The passphrase, the recovery code and a
device passkey on a *phrase-created* wallet all belong to that stored copy.

What gets you back in is the written recovery phrase or, on a passkey-created wallet, a passkey that
syncs. Keep one of those safe. The full matrix is in
[The Built-In Wallet](/wallets/built-in-wallet#site-data-and-your-backup).

Accounts held in Superhero Wallet or in an agent's environment variable are unaffected.

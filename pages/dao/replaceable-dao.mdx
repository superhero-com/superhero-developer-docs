# Replaceable DAOs

Sophia contracts are immutable once deployed, and Superhero's contracts run without a proxy or
an admin key, so the rules a community lives under are the code it voted for. A community that
wants different governance rules deploys a **different DAO contract** and votes to hand over
control. `ReplaceableDAO.aes` defines the interface that makes that possible, and `ChangeDAO`
is the vote that performs it.

What survives a migration: the AEX-9 token keeps its address, its supply and its holders; the
bonding curve keeps its reserve and its price; the community keeps its name and its posts.
Only the contract that owns the sale and holds the treasury is replaced.

## The interface

A contract that implements all three entrypoints can be the target of a `ChangeDAO` vote:

| Entrypoint | Signature | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `is_dao` | `() : bool` | Must return `true`. Confirms the target is a DAO before anything moves. |
| `get_community_management` | `() : option(CommunityManagement)` | The linked community management contract, or `None`. |
| `record_sale_transaction` | `(address, int) : unit`, stateful, payable | Receive sale proceeds with the buyer and buy price as arguments. |

The stock `DAO.aes` implements all three, so a community's DAO can be replaced by another
instance of the same implementation as well as by a custom one.

### `record_sale_transaction`

```text
stateful payable entrypoint record_sale_transaction(buyer: address, buy_price: int) : unit
```

The AE arrives with the call; the **buyer** and the **buy price** arrive as arguments. A
replacement DAO can therefore act on income the moment it lands: split it between a treasury
and a rewards pool, credit it per buyer, forward a share to a partner contract. The token sale
calls it with `protected = true`, so a trade completes even if the DAO call aborts, and calls it
with zero value as a probe on `set_beneficiary`, so the entrypoint must accept zero-value calls.

### `is_dao`

`ChangeDAO` transfers ownership of the token sale, the beneficiary rights to all future
proceeds, and the accumulated treasury, in one call. `is_dao()` returning `true` is a type
check that the target is at least a DAO-shaped contract: an account address, the token
address or an unrelated contract does not answer the call. It is a shape check, since any
contract can implement `is_dao() = true`, so verify the target's source yourself before voting.

## Build a replacement DAO

1. **Write the contract in Sophia.** Implement `is_dao`, `get_community_management` and
   `record_sale_transaction` with exactly the signatures above. Sophia type-checks the
   interface, so a mismatched arity or a missing `payable` fails at the call site.
2. **Decide what it does with authority.** The replacement becomes the owner and beneficiary
   of the existing `AffiliationBondingCurveTokenSale`, and the owner of the existing
   `CommunityManagement`. Expose equivalents of `add_vote` and `apply_vote_subject` so the
   community keeps the ability to appoint moderators and change its write threshold; those
   actions are reached through the sale's owner.
3. **Point it at the existing `CommunityManagement`.** `apply_vote_subject` asserts
   `get_community_management() == new_dao.get_community_management()` and aborts the whole
   migration with `NEW_DAO_HAS_TO_REFERENCE_SAME_COMMUNITY_MANAGEMENT` if they differ. The
   stock DAO derives it by asking its factory (`factory.get_community_management(token_sale)`),
   so a replacement built the same way needs the factory address and the sale address at
   deployment.
4. **Deploy it** to the same network, and check that `is_dao()` answers `true` from an
   external call before proposing anything.
5. **Start it empty.** The treasury transfers with the migration.
6. **Publish the source and the bytecode hash** so voters can verify that the deployed
   contract at the proposed address is the code they are being asked to approve.

## The migration

A `ChangeDAO` proposal is an ordinary DAO vote:

1. A holder at or above the community threshold calls `DAO.add_vote(metadata)` with
   `subject = ChangeDAO(ct_new_dao...)`, paying 555 AE.
2. The vote is open for two weeks.
3. It needs at least 55% of the voted stake in favour and at least 10% of total supply
   participating.
4. Within roughly two days of closing, somebody calls `DAO.apply_vote_subject(vote_seq_id)`.
5. The old DAO verifies `is_dao()` on the target, then transfers the treasury, the sale's
   beneficiary and owner, and ownership of `CommunityManagement`. On the way it checks that the
   target references the same community management contract.

Apply it inside the window: a vote that passes its window expires, the 555 AE stays with the
treasury, and the migration needs a new proposal.

### What the migration transfers

| Thing | Transferred? | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The AE treasury | Yes, in full | `Chain.spend(new_dao_address, Contract.balance)` |
| Token sale beneficiary | Yes | `get_token_sale().set_beneficiary(new_dao_address)` |
| Token sale ownership | Yes | `get_token_sale().set_owner(new_dao_address)` |
| `CommunityManagement` ownership | Yes | `community_management.set_owner(new_dao_address)` |
| The moderator roster, mute list, threshold and metadata | Untouched | They live in `CommunityManagement`, which is not redeployed |
| The AEX-9 token, its supply and its holders | Untouched | Not owned by the DAO |
| The bonding curve and its reserve | Untouched | Held by the sale contract, which is not replaced |
| The vote history | Stays on the old DAO | `DAO.votes` stays on the old contract, which keeps answering `votes()` |

Because `CommunityManagement.set_owner` is called in the same transaction, the
`AddModerator`, `DeleteModerator`, `ChangeMinimumTokenThreshold` and `ChangeMetaInfo`
subjects keep working after a migration, provided the replacement DAO implements equivalents
of `add_vote` and `apply_vote_subject` to reach them.

After a migration, read older votes from the old DAO and newer ones from the new DAO, whose
vote ids start at zero.

## Before voting on a `ChangeDAO` proposal

- **The handover completes in one transaction.** Treasury, sale ownership and beneficiary
  rights move together at execution.
- **Reversal goes through the new DAO.** Undoing a migration needs the *new* DAO to offer a
  `ChangeDAO` equivalent.
- **`is_dao()` is a shape check.** Verify the contract behind the address, since any contract
  can return `true`.
- **The vote approves an address.** Check that the source is published and that the deployed
  bytecode at the proposed address matches it.
- **Governance becomes whatever the replacement implements.** Read its proposal and voting
  entrypoints and find out who controls it.

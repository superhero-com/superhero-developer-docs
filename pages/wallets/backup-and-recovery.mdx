# Backup & Recovery

Read this before you put value in an account. Superhero accounts are self-sovereign: you hold the
key, and what you keep is what restores the account.

## The one-minute version

| You use | Back up | Keep it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superhero Wallet (extension, mobile or web) | The seed phrase. Reveal it under Settings → Seed phrase after setup | Offline, on paper or metal, in two places |
| A Ledger or AirGap account inside Superhero Wallet | The device's own recovery words | Wherever that device's maker tells you |
| An account imported into Superhero Wallet by private key | That private key | Offline, on its own; the key is what restores it |
| Built-in wallet created **from a phrase** | The 12 words, plus the recovery code | Offline; keep both, the words restore anywhere |
| Built-in wallet created **from a passkey** | Confirm your passkey **syncs**; keep the recovery code | Sync is the backup; the code unlocks on this device |
| An agent key (`AE_PRIVATE_KEY`) | The secret key string | Offline, never in git, never in a log |

## Back up a seed phrase

1. **Write it down by hand.** Keep it out of screenshots and text files, which sync to the cloud
   and get indexed.
2. **Copy it exactly**: all 12 or 24 words, in order, lowercase. BIP39 phrases carry a checksum,
   so a wrong or transposed word is usually caught on import.
3. **Verify it.** Superhero's built-in wallet has you re-enter the words before a vault is
   created. In the standalone Superhero Wallet, verify it under Settings → Seed phrase (see
   below).
4. **Store two copies in different physical places.**
5. **Never type it into anything but the wallet.** No support agent, no "validator", no airdrop
   checker will ever need it. See [Security Best Practices](/wallets/security).

The phrase is a standard BIP39 phrase on the standard æternity derivation path
(`m/44'/457'/<index>'/0'/0'`, SLIP-0010 ed25519). The same phrase produces byte-identical
addresses in the Superhero Wallet extension, the native app, and Superhero's built-in wallet.

## Superhero Wallet: reveal, verify, export

**Reveal the phrase after onboarding.** Onboarding generates a mnemonic, creates your first account
and goes straight to the dashboard. A notification ("Welcome to Superhero Wallet! Please backup
your seed phrase!" with a **Back up now** button) reminds you until the phrase is marked backed up.

**Revealing it takes re-authentication.** Settings → Seed phrase → **Show seed phrase**:

| Situation | What you have to do |
| --- | --- |
| Mobile app with biometrics on | Pass a biometric check |
| Extension or web with a password set | Re-enter the password |
| Mobile with biometrics off, or password protection turned off | Confirm a "make sure no one is watching your screen" dialog |

**Verify it.** *Verify seed phrase* shows your own words shuffled for you to tap back into the
correct order; *I have verified seed phrase before* marks the step done. **Write the phrase down on
your first day.**

### Export one account's private key

Account details → **Show account private key** reveals the active account's secret key as hex,
with a copy button, behind the same re-authentication as the phrase reveal.

It exports **one account**, not the wallet. Use it to move a single account into another wallet
that supports private-key import. An account you *imported* by private key is restored with that
private key, so back it up on its own.

In the built-in wallet, the backup is the written phrase (phrase-created) or the syncing passkey
(passkey-created); see below.

### Change the wallet password

Settings → Secure Login has a change-password form: it verifies the current password, then
re-encrypts the stored seed under the new one. The same screen turns password protection off and
sets the auto-lock interval: immediately, 1, 5, 15 or 30 minutes of inactivity.

## Back up the built-in wallet

The built-in wallet has three unlock factors, and each does a different job:

| Factor | Unlocks on this device | Restores on a new device |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passphrase | Yes | No |
| Recovery code | Yes | No |
| Syncing passkey (on a passkey-created wallet) | Yes | **Yes** |
| Device-bound passkey (on a passkey-created wallet) | Yes | No |
| Passkey on a **phrase-created** wallet | Yes | No |
| The written recovery phrase | n/a | **Yes, anywhere** |

The passphrase and the recovery code each unwrap a key inside this device's encrypted vault
record. Two things reconstruct a seed from scratch: the written phrase and, for a wallet created by
the passkey path, the passkey itself, because that seed is *derived* from the passkey's PRF output
at a fixed public salt.

- **Phrase-created built-in wallet**: the 12 words are your portable backup. The recovery code is a
  same-device fallback for a forgotten passphrase or a rekeyed passkey. Keep both.
- **Passkey-created built-in wallet**: the passkey *is* the backup when it syncs. Confirm it landed
  in iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager; a device-bound credential such as Windows Hello
  stays on that device and is the copy of the seed, so use a syncing one. Enroll the recovery code
  as well: it is the factor that survives a passkey rekey.

## Recover on a new device

### Superhero Wallet (extension, mobile, web)

Install it, choose **Restore existing wallet**, and enter your seed phrase: 12 or more words,
separated by spaces, in order. You can also scan the phrase from a QR code. The wallet validates
the phrase, restores your accounts and re-derives their keys.

Two kinds of account are restored from their own secret, because the wallet stored their address
rather than their key:

- **Ledger and AirGap accounts.** Re-import them from the device.
- **Accounts imported by private key.** Restore those from the key you exported.

### Built-in wallet, from a recovery phrase

Open the installed Superhero app (import is offered in the installed PWA), choose to import
an existing wallet, and paste the phrase. It is validated against the BIP39 wordlist and checksum
before anything is stored, then sealed under a new passphrase on the new device.

### Built-in wallet, from a passkey

Choose **"Restore a wallet you created with a passkey."** This runs a discoverable WebAuthn
assertion, derives the wallet the credential implies, and **shows you the derived address before
saving anything.** The confirm screen also checks that address for on-chain activity:

- **"This account has on-chain activity"**: a genuine restore. Confirm.
- **"No on-chain activity"**: stop and go back. You most likely selected a passkey belonging to a
  *phrase-created* wallet, which derives a different, empty wallet. Confirming would save that
  wallet on this device.
- **"Couldn't check on-chain activity"**: the activity check did not complete. Judge by the address
  itself.

### An agent key

The secret key is the account:

```bash
export AE_PRIVATE_KEY=<your_secret_key>
node scripts/superhero-wallet.mjs address
node scripts/superhero-wallet.mjs balance
```

## What to keep safe

- **Your seed phrase.** You alone hold it; nobody else has a copy to hand back, so keep it where
  you can find it.
- **A written phrase or a syncing passkey for the built-in wallet.** Clearing site data for
  superhero.com removes the IndexedDB vault, and Safari's Intelligent Tracking Prevention can
  remove it after about seven days of no interaction. The passphrase and recovery code unlock the
  vault on this device; the phrase or a syncing passkey restores it anywhere.
- **A syncing passkey for a passkey-created wallet.** Confirm the credential is in iCloud Keychain
  or Google Password Manager before you rely on it.
- **The address you send to.** Transactions are final, so check it before signing.
- **Your private key, out of every log, chat and repository.** If one was logged, pasted or
  committed, move the funds to a fresh account.

If the built-in wallet's address list in local storage is cleared while the encrypted vault
survives, unlock once and the app re-derives your addresses from index 0 upward.

## A backup routine that holds

- Write the phrase down and read it back **before** funding the account.
- Store two copies in two places.
- Store the recovery code separately from the phrase. They cover different cases, so keep them in different places.
- If you rely on a passkey, verify it is present on a *second* device before you trust it as a
  backup.
- Do a restore drill once: import into a second wallet and confirm the first address matches.

## Changing the built-in wallet's passphrase

The built-in wallet's factors are set when the wallet is created, so you change them by recreating
the wallet.

- **To change your passphrase, recreate the wallet.** Unlock with any enrolled factor, write down
  the recovery phrase, erase the wallet, and create a new one with the same phrase and a new
  passphrase. You get a new recovery code; the account and its address are unchanged, because they
  come from the seed.
- **If someone has seen a factor, recreate the wallet the same day.** Any one factor opens the
  vault, and recreating the wallet is how you retire the old one.

The standalone Superhero Wallet also offers a change-password form under Settings → Secure Login.

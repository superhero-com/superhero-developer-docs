# Risks

Community tokens are volatile, reflexive, and structurally loss-making on a short round trip. The
bonding curve removes some risks that exist elsewhere (there is no liquidity to pull and no
counterparty to default) and introduces others that are specific to it.

Nothing here is financial advice. Never risk funds you cannot afford to lose.

## 1. Every position starts about 1% down

You buy at the curve price plus a 0.5% affiliation fee. You can only sell at 0.995 times the curve
price. An instant round trip at unchanged supply therefore returns `0.995 / 1.005 = 0.99004975…` of what
you paid:

```text
buy 1,000 tokens at supply 10,000,000
  curve integral                   0.105276443949 AE
  × 1.005 affiliation fee          0.105802826168 AE   ← you pay this

sell them straight back
  × 0.995 sell factor              0.104750061729 AE   ← you receive this

loss                               0.001052764439 AE   = 0.995% of what you paid
```

Before gas. **Your sell return must increase by about 1.005% to recover the purchase cost.**
The additional supply needed depends on your entry supply and position size; a 1% price
increase does not imply a 1% supply increase on this curve.

For a trader doing many round trips this compounds: ten round trips at unchanged prices leave you
with `(0.995 / 1.005)^10 ≈ 0.90484` of your capital, a 9.52% loss from friction alone. Frequent trading on a 1%
spread loses unless each move is substantially larger than 1%.

## 2. The exit price is not the quoted price

A holdings row or a market cap figure prices your tokens at the current spot price. Selling walks the
curve down as your tokens burn, so what you receive is the integral, not the rectangle. For a token
with 10,000,000 supply:

| Your balance | Share of supply | Quoted value (AE) | Actual sell return (AE) | Shortfall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000 | 1% | 10.47 | 10.42 | 0.5% |
| 1,000,000 | 10% | 104.74 | 99.26 | 5.2% |
| 5,000,000 | 50% | 523.72 | 388.53 | 25.8% |

**The larger your position relative to the token, the more the displayed value overstates your
exit.** Call `sell_return(balance)` on the sale contract for the exact number before you plan around
a position's value. See [Portfolio & PnL](/tokens/portfolio).

The same logic makes large entries expensive: adding 10% to a token's supply costs about 5% above
the price you were quoted, and doubling the supply costs about 54% above it. Splitting the order
does not reduce this price impact in the continuous model. Integer rounding and per-transaction
network fees can change exact totals. See [Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

## 3. Reflexivity

Price is a pure function of supply and nothing else. There is no external market, no arbitrageur,
and no fundamental value pulling the price back to anything, so the feedback loop is direct: buying
raises the price, which attracts buyers, which raises the price, and selling does the same in
reverse. Neither direction has a natural floor above the curve itself, and a price that rose on one
large buy is exactly as "real" as one that rose on a thousand small ones.

**Momentum signals describe buying that has already moved the price.** By the time a token tops the
trending list, the supply that made it trend has already been minted, and you would be buying
further up the curve than the people the signal is reporting on. See
[The Trending Score](/tokens/trending-score).

## 4. Thin communities and concentration

Most community tokens are small. The specific dangers in a thin one:

- **A single holder can be the market.** If one address holds 50% of the supply, their exit walks the price down through every other holder's position first. Check the Holders tab before you buy.
- **The creator's entry was much cheaper than yours.** A pre-buy at creation happens at the bottom of the curve: the first 437,014 tokens have a curve cost of about 1 AE, while the same amount bought at a supply of 10,000,000 has a curve cost of about 47.06 AE. Both purchases also pay the affiliation fee and network charges. An early holder can be ahead while a later one is underwater.
- **Low holder count with high supply is one person's position with a price attached**, not a community.
- **Dilution.** Supply is unlimited and minted on demand. Your balance does not change when other people buy, but your share of the supply, and therefore your governance weight, falls.

## 5. Abandoned tokens

Nothing about a community token expires. If everybody loses interest, the contract keeps quoting
prices and the curve keeps working, but:

- There is no one to trade against except the curve, and no activity to move it.
- Whatever the DAO treasury accumulated stays there. Opening a DAO vote costs **555 AE**, paid into the same treasury. A passing `VotePayout` pays out the whole balance, deposit included, so a small treasury is still spendable; the proposer risks the 555 AE only if the vote fails or is not applied in time. See [DAO voting](/dao/voting).
- Governance requires a quorum of 10% of the token stake and 55% approval within two weeks. In an abandoned community that quorum may be unreachable, which freezes the treasury, the moderator set and the write threshold as they are.

The curve computes your sell quote from the remaining supply, at the price the last trades left
behind. Buys fund the reserve, but the continuous-model redemption argument needs separate
integer-rounding analysis. See [Reserve accounting and rounding](/concepts/value-flow#reserve-accounting-and-rounding).

## 6. Mechanism risk

| Risk | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Slippage between quote and fill | Other trades land before yours. Guarded by your slippage tolerance on a buy and by `minimal_return_required` on a sell. Both revert rather than fill badly, and a revert still costs gas |
| A sell with no floor | `minimal_return_required` of 0 means "fill me at any price". On a falling curve that is exactly wrong |
| Standing allowances | Selling requires granting the sale contract an AEX-9 allowance. Leaving a large one in place authorises that contract to move that many tokens until you reduce it |
| Two-step sells | In the app and CLI a sell is two transactions. If the second fails, you have granted an allowance and still hold the tokens |
| Front-running | Anyone watching the mempool can place a buy ahead of yours, pushing you further up the curve. Your tolerance bounds the damage; it does not prevent it |

## 7. Smart contract risk

The contracts are written in Sophia, a functional language with strong static typing and
contract-local mutable state. FATE rejects reentrant calls into an executing contract. The sale contract
retains the immediate sell quote on each buy, which funds full redemption in the ideal continuous
model. Integer truncation requires a separate check for exact solvency across trade sequences;
the continuous argument alone does not establish it for deployed contracts. The contracts have
not undergone a third-party audit.
See [Contract security](/contracts/security).

- **Bugs in the sale, curve, DAO or factory contracts.** These are immutable once deployed. A `ChangeDAO` vote can migrate a community's DAO, but the token and sale contracts are fixed.
- **Upgrade and governance paths.** The `ChangeDAO` vote subject transfers ownership, beneficiary rights and accumulated funds to another DAO contract. It requires 55% approval and 10% participation, but in a community where one holder controls a majority of the supply, that is one holder's decision.
- **Composability.** The sale contract depends on the bonding curve, the affiliation treasury, the factory and the token contract. A fault in any of them affects trading.

## 8. Operational risk

- **Your keys are the account.** There is no password reset, no support desk that can restore access, and no way to reverse a transfer. See [Backup and recovery](/wallets/backup-and-recovery).
- **Read the transaction before you sign it.** Check the contract address, the amount and the entrypoint. A malicious site can ask you to sign something that looks like a buy.
- **Phishing.** Addresses are long and look alike. Verify a sale address against the token page or [aeScan](https://aescan.io) rather than pasting one from a chat message.
- **Agents holding raw private keys.** The CLI scripts sign with `AE_PRIVATE_KEY` from the environment. An agent with that key can spend the whole balance, and a prompt-injected agent can be told to. Use a dedicated wallet with a limited balance. See [Agent safety](/agents/safety).
- **Autonomous trading.** A strategy that trades every 2–6 hours pays the 1% spread every round trip. Model the friction before you enable it. See [Autonomous mode](/agents/autonomous-mode).

## 9. Availability and data risk

The chain is the system of record; superhero.com is a client on top of it. If the web app or the
indexer is down, your tokens and the contracts are unaffected (you can trade directly against the
sale contract with the SDK), but price charts, the trending score, portfolio valuations and
transaction history are served by the backend and go with it. Indexer lag is normal: a new token
takes 2–5 minutes to appear, and a trade may be on-chain before it shows in a list. Verify against
the explorer before concluding something failed.

## What a community token is not

- **Not equity**, not a revenue share, and not a claim on any company's assets.
- The DAO treasury is spent by vote, not distributed. There is **no dividend**.

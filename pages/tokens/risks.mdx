# Before You Trade

Community tokens are priced by a curve, so a few things work differently from an order book. The
curve removes some risks that exist elsewhere (there is no liquidity to pull and no counterparty to
default) and has mechanics of its own that are worth knowing before you trade.

None of this is financial advice. Trade with funds you can afford to lose.

## 1. Every position starts about 1% down

You buy at the curve price plus a 0.5% affiliation fee, and you sell at 0.995 times the curve
price. An instant round trip at unchanged supply therefore returns `0.995 / 1.005 = 0.99502` of what
you paid:

```text
buy 1,000 tokens at supply 10,000,000
  curve integral                   0.105276443949 AE
  × 1.005 affiliation fee          0.105802826168 AE   ← you pay this

sell them straight back
  × 0.995 sell factor              0.104750061729 AE   ← you receive this

loss                               0.001052764439 AE   = 0.995% of what you paid
```

Before gas. **The price has to rise about 1% before you are level**, which on this curve means the
supply must grow by roughly the same 1%: somebody else has to buy about as much as you did for you
to break even.

For a trader doing many round trips this compounds: ten round trips at unchanged prices leave you
with `0.99502^10 = 0.951` of your capital, a 4.9% cost from the spread alone. Each move needs to be
larger than 1% for a round trip to come out ahead.

## 2. The exit price is the curve integral, not the spot price

A holdings row or a market cap figure prices your tokens at the current spot price. Selling walks the
curve down as your tokens burn, so what you receive is the integral, not the rectangle. For a token
with 10,000,000 supply:

| Your balance | Share of supply | Quoted value (AE) | Actual sell return (AE) | Shortfall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000 | 1% | 10.47 | 10.42 | 0.5% |
| 1,000,000 | 10% | 104.74 | 99.26 | 5.2% |
| 5,000,000 | 50% | 523.72 | 388.53 | 25.8% |

**The larger your position relative to the supply, the further the exit sits below the displayed
value.** Call `sell_return(balance)` on the sale contract for the exact number before you plan around
a position's value. See [Portfolio & PnL](/tokens/portfolio).

The same arithmetic applies to large entries: adding 10% to a token's supply costs about 5% above
the price you were quoted, and doubling the supply costs about 54% above it. The curve is
path-independent, so splitting the order gives the same total. See
[Fees & Slippage](/tokens/fees-and-slippage).

## 3. Price follows supply

Price is a pure function of supply. There is no external market or arbitrageur pulling it toward
anything else, so the feedback is direct: buying raises the price and selling lowers it. A price
that rose on one large buy is the same price as one that rose on a thousand small ones; the
transaction list tells you which it was.

**Momentum signals describe buying that has already moved the price.** By the time a token tops the
trending list, the supply that made it trend has already been minted, and you buy further up the
curve than the people the signal is reporting on. See [The Trending Score](/tokens/trending-score).

## 4. Concentration and dilution

Most community tokens are small. What to check in a thin one:

- **The Holders tab.** If one address holds 50% of the supply, their exit walks the price down through every other holder's position first.
- **The creator's entry.** A pre-buy at creation happens at the very bottom of the curve: the first 437,014 tokens cost exactly 1 AE, while the same 437,014 tokens bought at a supply of 10,000,000 cost 47.06 AE, 47 times more. Early holders bought far below the current price.
- **Holder count against supply.** Many holders with a modest supply is a community; one or two holders with a large supply is a single position.
- **Dilution.** Supply is minted on demand. Your balance does not change when other people buy, but your share of the supply, and therefore your governance weight, falls.

## 5. Protecting a trade

| Guard | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Slippage tolerance | Other trades can land before yours. Your slippage tolerance on a buy and `minimal_return_required` on a sell bound the fill. Both revert rather than fill outside the bound; a revert pays gas |
| A sell floor | `minimal_return_required` of 0 means "fill me at any price". Set a floor from the quote |
| Allowances | Selling grants the sale contract an AEX-9 allowance. Set it to the amount you are selling and reduce it afterwards |
| Two-step sells | A sell is two transactions. If the second reverts, the allowance stays granted and you still hold the tokens; retry the sell or reset the allowance |
| Front-running | Anyone watching the mempool can place a buy ahead of yours. Your tolerance bounds how far up the curve you fill |

## 6. The contracts

The contracts are written in Sophia, a functional language with strong static typing and no mutable
global state, which narrows the surface for reentrancy and state-corruption bugs. The economic design
is solvent by construction: the sell curve sits strictly below the buy curve at every supply, so the
reserve can always redeem the entire supply. See [Contract security](/contracts/security).

- **Immutability.** The token and sale contracts are fixed once deployed. A `ChangeDAO` vote can migrate a community's DAO.
- **Governance paths.** The `ChangeDAO` vote subject transfers ownership, beneficiary rights and accumulated funds to another DAO contract. It requires 55% approval and 10% participation; in a community where one holder controls a majority of the supply, that holder carries the vote.
- **Composition.** The sale contract works with the bonding curve, the affiliation treasury, the factory and the token contract.

## 7. Keys and signing

- **Your keys are the account.** Keep a backup: access is restored from your seed phrase, and a signed transfer is final. See [Backup and recovery](/wallets/backup-and-recovery).
- **Read the transaction before you sign it.** Check the contract address, the amount and the entrypoint. A malicious site can ask you to sign something that looks like a buy.
- **Phishing.** Addresses are long and look alike. Verify a sale address against the token page or [aeScan](https://aescan.io) rather than pasting one from a chat message.
- **Agents holding raw private keys.** The CLI scripts sign with `AE_PRIVATE_KEY` from the environment. An agent with that key can spend the whole balance, and a prompt-injected agent can be told to. Use a dedicated wallet with a limited balance. See [Agent safety](/agents/safety).
- **Autonomous trading.** A strategy that trades every 2–6 hours pays the 1% spread every round trip. Model the friction before you enable it. See [Autonomous mode](/agents/autonomous-mode).

## 8. The chain is the system of record

superhero.com is a client on top of the chain. Your tokens and the contracts live on-chain, and you
can always trade directly against the sale contract with the SDK. Price charts, the trending score,
portfolio valuations and transaction history are served by the backend. A new token takes 2–5 minutes
to appear after it is mined, and a trade can be on-chain before it shows in a list. Verify against the
explorer before concluding something failed.

## What a community token represents

- A governance stake and community membership, backed by the AE in its own bonding-curve reserve. It is not equity and carries no claim on any company's assets.
- A treasury that holders spend by vote. Nothing is distributed automatically.
